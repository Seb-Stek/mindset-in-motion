About us

One idea. A lot of happy kids.

Mindset in Motion teaches kids ages 8 to 12 valuable skills in sports and in everyday life. We run free clinics that build skills on the field and a positive mindset for everything else.

Gear in hand at the Boys & Girls Clubs of Union County

How it started

A lot of kids never get the chance to play organized sports. We wanted to change that, one hour at a time.

In the summer of 2026, Sebastian Steketee, a high school soccer and tennis player, decided to do something about it. His idea was simple: bring free clinics to the kids, teach them the game, and teach them the mindset behind it. He designed the clinic, wrote the five rules, and reached out to find a partner to host the first event.

His teammate Boris joined as a volunteer coach for one of the first events and stuck around. Today he's Sebastian's right hand as vice president.

That mindset is the heart of what we do. Shaking off a mistake, staying positive when you're losing, being a good sport: the habits that help you in sports help you everywhere else too. Play better, live better.

We packed up balls, cones and pop-up goals and teamed up with the Boys & Girls Clubs of Union County. Our first event, on August 10, was an hour of soccer games and races outside on the field. One week later we were back, this time inside their gym.

Now we're planning what comes next: more events, more community partners, and more sports.

What we do

Free one-hour clinics for kids ages 8 to 12, in two parts: developing sports skills, then developing life skills.

Always free

Every event is free, for the kids and for the organizations that host us. We're not here to make money. We're here to make a difference.

We bring the gear

Balls, cones, pop-up goals, agility hurdles and pinnies. Our hosts just need a field or a gym, and the kids.

Sports skills

Hands-on, individual coaching in ball control, dribbling, passing, shooting and field positioning. Then a fun game to put it all to work.

Life skills

A talk about the mental side of the game: mindfulness, sportsmanship, winning and losing, perseverance, and letting go of the past.

Five things to always remember

Every clinic ends with a talk about the mental side of the game. These are the five things we want every kid to take home.

1

Shake it off

Be mindful

Focus on the present moment, and you'll have less stress. Everybody misses, even the best players in the world. When you mess up, don't dwell on it. Shake it off and go get the next ball.

Life takeaway: Don't be hard on yourself. Focus on the next play, and the next opportunity life gives you.

2

Keep going

Be persistent

No matter how many times you fail, success is right around the corner. Cristiano Ronaldo has scored almost 1,000 goals, but he misses about 5 of every 6 shots he takes. He just keeps shooting.

Life takeaway: The harder you work, the more you'll succeed. Your hard work will pay off.

3

Switch it up

Be flexible

Doing well takes the ability to adapt. If there's a wall in front of you, you don't keep running into it. You go around it. If going right isn't working, go left.

Life takeaway: If you're flexible, you can adapt to any situation and make it a positive experience.

4

Good game

Be a good sport

As Sebastian's grandmother says, “Always win with grace and lose with dignity.” No bragging when you win, no throwing a fit when you lose. Shake hands, say good game, and mean it.

Life takeaway: If things don't go your way, keep your head up. New opportunities are right around the corner.

5

Winners learn

Be a winner

A winner is just someone who lost and tried one more time. Every time you lose, you learn one thing to get better at. Win or lose, learn from it, and you'll always be a winner.

Life takeaway: Losing can bring a lot of growth.
